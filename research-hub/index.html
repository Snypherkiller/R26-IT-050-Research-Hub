CEYLONGO RESEARCH

Our research

CeylonGo is a research prototype developed to support tourism planning in Sri Lanka by connecting accommodation and activity selection, personalised itinerary planning, route and vehicle advice, and landmark information. The project addresses the difficulty of combining fragmented travel information with individual preferences and practical journey constraints. Its implementation combines a React Native/Expo application, Express and Flask services, Neo4j graph retrieval, machine-learning models, and external information services. Natural-language requests support hotel-centred package suggestions; a relevance classifier and heuristic optimisation support itinerary construction; historical hazard classification informs route and vehicle recommendations; and image recognition links supported landmarks to contextual information and an optional conversational guide. Stored experiments provide separate evidence for route-severity classification, user–place relevance, and landmark recognition. These results measure different tasks and do not establish a single platform accuracy. Important limitations include incomplete data provenance, restricted geographic coverage, differences between landmark training and serving, and missing end-to-end user and field evaluations. The contribution is an integrated decision-support prototype whose recommendations expose constraints and uncertainty, with further validation required before broader operational use.

Research domain

Problem & research gap

Travellers must reconcile accommodation, activities, journey time, transport suitability and landmark information across separate sources. Our research addresses the integration of these decisions while preserving preferences and communicating uncertainty.

The contribution is a connected decision-support prototype. A systematic review establishing field-wide novelty has not yet been documented.

Objectives

Develop and examine a unified Sri Lankan tourism prototype that connects package selection, itinerary planning, mobility advice and landmark information.

  • Connect four research components through shared interfaces.
  • Preserve traveller preferences and practical constraints.
  • Explain recommendations using structured evidence.
  • Evaluate component performance and identify deployment limitations.
Literature survey & research foundations

The project reports discuss retrieval-augmented generation, graph retrieval, gradient boosting, road routing, relevance classification, heuristic optimisation and landmark recognition.

  • Lewis et al. (2020), Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks.
  • Friedman (2001), Greedy Function Approximation: A Gradient Boosting Machine.
  • Geisberger et al. (2008), Contraction Hierarchies: Faster and Simpler Hierarchical Routing in Road Networks.
  • Sandler et al. (2018), MobileNetV2: Inverted Residuals and Linear Bottlenecks.
  • Cortes and Vapnik (1995), Support-Vector Networks.
  • Dosovitskiy et al. (2021), An Image Is Worth 16×16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale.

Methodology & research components

Prototype development combines structured tourism data, graph queries, classifiers, optimisation and optional language generation. GraphRAG is not applied uniformly to all components.

Intelligent Tour Package Generation

IT22895196

Natural-language preferences → validated Neo4j queries → ranked hotel and activity suggestions. Ollama assists preference extraction; package assembly uses deterministic rules.

Limitation: Activity-data ingestion and travel-time feasibility require further validation.

Context-Aware Itinerary Optimizer

IT22224866

Interests, location and available time → relevance ranking and genetic-algorithm selection → road-time scheduling with OSRM. The current catalogue covers 40 places in Kandy, Matale and Nuwara Eliya.

Limitation: Visit durations are estimates; opening windows are supplied by users. Field-trip timing remains unvalidated.

Intelligent Mobility, Route-Risk & Vehicle Selection

IT22555458

Route, passengers and budget → historical evidence retrieval and Gradient Boosting classification → vehicle shortlist and cost estimate.

Limitation: Historical severity is not accident probability. Exact road-segment matching and independent safety validation remain outstanding.

AI Landmark Recognition & Tourist Information

IT22214720

Image → MobileNetV2 features with SVM or TFLite inference → one of 17 landmark classes and contextual information. An optional Gemini guide supports questions.

Limitation: Training and serving pipelines need reconciliation; recognition of unknown landmarks and guide factuality remain unmeasured.

System architecture

React Native / Expo application → Express and Flask services → Neo4j, MongoDB and local CSV/model artifacts. External providers support routing, geocoding, weather, hotel offers and optional language generation.

Data and evaluation approach
  • Packages: 1,830 hotel rows and 2,000 activity rows; an activity-loader filename mismatch remains.
  • Itinerary: 40 source-traced points of interest; v1 evaluation uses 60 human-reviewed pairs from held-out Kandy profiles.
  • Mobility: 598 derived training records across 22 route groups; only 135 records have matching road profiles. Evaluation reserves unseen route groups.
  • Landmarks: notebook records 5,500 images with a 4,400/1,100 training/validation split across 17 classes. Raw collection provenance needs confirmation.

Conclusions & next steps

The prototype connects complementary tourism decisions and provides component-level experimental evidence. An integrated user study, measured field-trip timing, package relevance benchmark, chatbot factuality evaluation and independent safety evaluation are still needed.

Future work includes authoritative data sourcing, broader geographic coverage, exact road matching, consistent landmark training and serving, independent test sets and recorded end-to-end trials.
